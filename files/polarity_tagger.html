olarity Tagger
When typing: write the words the grammar needs, even where English leaves them out.
  • A bare plural or mass noun takes a determiner: write the one you mean
  • A command takes its subject: you don't leave a tip for don't leave a tip
  • A bare numeral takes a determiner: write the one you mean
  • A generic a, meaning every member of the kind, is written every: every lie has no legs for a lie has no legs

Lexicon

Each class pairs a CCG category with a marking-enriched type; every word in the class gets that pair. A word listed in more than one class loads as a switch card (⇄): tap its category to change the reading. The first class listed is the default.

Test set

Each sentence is shown as its cards: one card per word, after a few fixed changes.

  • Some words are added, because the grammar needs them: some before a bare plural or mass noun (some theorems), you as the subject of a command, exactly before a bare numeral (exactly two bananas).
  • A generic a is written as every: every lie has no legs.
  • Some word groups are one card: more than, less than, at least, at most, for free, got in, peanut butter.
  • everyone and anyone are two cards: every one, any one.
  • did not is the card not; didn't, doesn't and don't are one card each.
